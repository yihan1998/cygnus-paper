\subsection{Scheduling Policies in the Wild}
\label{sec:background-scheduling}

Scheduling policies differ in two main aspects:
whether preemption is allowed, and how the next task to run is selected.
Based on these two dimensions, we can categorize scheduling policies into four quadrants:
(1) non-preemptive scheduling with non-priority based selection policies (e.g., FCFS), 
(2) non-preemptive scheduling with priority-based policies (e.g., SRPT), 
(3) preemptive scheduling with non-priority based policies (e.g., Round Robin), and 
(4) preemptive scheduling with priority-based policies (e.g., Earliest Deadline First).

Among these policies, non-preemptive scheduling is the easiest to implement, as it does not require the ability to interrupt a running task.

Preemptive scheduling, on the other hand, requires the ability to interrupt a running task and switch to another task, which is harder to express in the scheduling policy.
We focus on three main categories of scheduling policies in this domain:
(1) Size-based policies, which prioritize tasks based on their expected service time (e.g., SRPT), 
(2) Age-based policies, which prioritize tasks based on their waiting time (e.g., FCFS), and 
(3) Deadline-based policies, which prioritize tasks based on their deadlines (e.g., EDF).
