Modern datacenter applications like social networking, financial trading systems, and online video conferencing operate under extremely stringent, microsecond-scale Service Level Objectives (SLOs).
Meanwhile, these applications exhibit highly diverse workload characteristics, encompassing a wide range of request arrival patterns and service time distributions.

The diversity in SLOs and workload characteristics across applications necessitates the need for tailored scheduling policies to meet specific application requirements.

However, enforcing application-specific scheduling policies at microsecond-scale remains challenging.
Monolithic kernels provide only a handful of scheduling policies, prioritizing generality over per-application requirements.
Prior work~\cite{humphries2021ghost,linux-sched-ext} improves kernel scheduling flexibility through extensible scheduling classes, but remains constrained by the kernel’s millisecond-scale scheduling granularity.
To avoid kernel scheduling overhead, recent research has proposed highly specialized dataplane operating systems (OSes) for microsecond-scale workloads.
Despite their performance benefits, these systems conflate a one-size-fits-all scheduling policy deep in the runtime, making it impractical for applications to customize scheduling without modifying the underlying OS.

In this paper, we present \sysname, a dataplane OS architecture that introduces programmable scheduling abstractions for application-defined policies with microsecond-scale enforcement.
Our key insight is that the rigidity of state-of-the-art dataplane OSes arises from \textit{conflating scheduling policy with scheduling mechanism}.
Thus, \sysname aims to decouple these two concerns:
application developers solely define the scheduling policy, while the dataplane OS provides the mechanisms needed to enforce it at microsecond-scale.
