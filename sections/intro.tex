Scheduling plays a central role in meeting the diverse Service Level Objectives (SLOs) of modern datacenter applications.

Achieving these SLOs requires both a \textit{scheduling policy}, which determines how tasks are ordered, and a specification of \textit{intent}, which captures the SLOs the scheduler is expected to satisfy.

However, translating policy and intent onto low-level scheduling mechanisms, such as timers and I/O events, to produce a correct scheduler remains laborious.

Prior work has proposed efficient scheduling frameworks that simplify policy deployment~\cite{humphries2021ghost, linux-sched-ext}, but still requires developers to manually bridge the implementation gap between high-level policy and intent and low-level mechanisms.

In this paper, we present \sysname, a scheduling synthesis framework that takes application-defined policy, intent, and available mechanisms as input and automatically synthesizes the corresponding scheduling implementation.
